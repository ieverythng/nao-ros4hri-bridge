The six passing knowledge requirements cover current visibility, scene updates, support relations, object naming, counting, and colour. The red-cup update changed symbolic state, but it produced an execution route, a planner request, and the \code{kb_add} and \code{report_result} operations. It therefore fails the requirement that knowledge maintenance remain outside the robot-action planner.

All four single-skill cases passed. Four composite behaviours also passed: ordered head motion, delivery of visible objects to ALEX, navigation to ALEX followed by a wave and completion report, and visiting every object with per-target reports. The recorded wave case uses the label \code{wave_greet}; the canonical twelve-skill taxonomy represents the same behaviour as a named \code{perform_motion}. The trace supports the observed behaviour but does not independently validate that registry projection.

The other failure occurred in the request to go to the kitchen and bring the cup to the operator. Its trace selected the kitchen as \code{recipient_id}, asked a clarification, and produced no execution feedback. That behaviour was safe because no unsupported action was dispatched, but it did not preserve the required location, object, and human-recipient roles and did not complete the task.

\section{Skill-Execution and Failure-Management Results}

The controlled skill-execution set exercised the same planner, orchestrator, feedback, and result contracts used by robot-facing skills. Deterministic adapters supplied repeatable outcomes at individual calls. Seven of the nine integrated cases passed. One omitted a phone from an \enquote{every object} target set, and one performed the expected missing-object search and help request but did not produce terminal closure. Both are failures under the stated acceptance criteria.

The retained traces also distinguish failure position within a goal. The primary beginning-stage probe failed the first navigation call, advanced the plan version, preserved all four targets, completed the revised execution, and closed successfully. Additional controlled observations provide the middle- and later-stage evidence: a pick probe first completed object finding, failed \code{pick_object} at the second step, and then executed a revised sequence containing scan, find, pick, and report; a blocked-delivery probe failed after preparatory finding and scanning, produced replanning, but lacked terminal closure. Because these observations were not part of the frozen primary matrix, they are used qualitatively rather than included in the aggregate counts of Table~\ref{tab:final-evidence-scope}. End-stage weakness is therefore evidenced by missing final reporting rather than by a dedicated last-skill failure injection.

\section{Answers to the Research Questions}

\textbf{RQ1---Can the architecture produce coherent plans?}
Yes, for the bounded tested families. The end-to-end questionnaire passed all four single-skill cases and four of five composite cases; the capability-extreme profile passed five of seven. Incomplete capability-extreme plans were rejected rather than partially executed.

\textbf{RQ2---Can it preserve grounded entities and symbolic state?}
Yes within the controlled fixtures: the environment profile passed 11/11 and the stateful KB chain passed 7/7. The kitchen recipient-role error and universal-selection omissions show that complete source context does not guarantee correct model-authored selection.

\textbf{RQ3---Can generated actions be constrained by reviewed interfaces?}
Yes for the tested schemas and registry. Invalid or incomplete plans did not cross the orchestrator boundary, and the model did not introduce an unregistered executable skill.

\textbf{RQ4---Can execution failure be handled safely?}
Beginning-stage navigation and middle-stage pick failures demonstrated valid feedback-driven replanning. Later blocked-delivery and missing-object traces remained less consistent, particularly in terminal closure and user-facing clarification.

\textbf{RQ5---Does the modular separation improve auditability and reuse?}
Yes at the software-contract level. The same dialogue, grounding, plan, feedback, and result boundaries were retained across vLLM, Ollama, deterministic simulated adapters, and the NAO adapters. Equivalent behaviour on another robot or under another model still requires its own qualification.

\section{Result Summary}

The frozen evidence supports a qualified but bounded conclusion. Under the primary configuration, 64 of 72 records passed and eight failed the applicable acceptance criteria. The end-to-end questionnaire produced 20 passes and two failures across 22 exercised requirements. The stack handled beginning- and middle-stage skill failures with valid replans, rejected incomplete capability-extreme plans before execution, and preserved coherent symbolic state across the controlled KB chain. Remaining errors were concentrated in model-authored route choice, universal target selection, role preservation, clarification wording, and terminal closure.

The language model remained non-deterministic. The principal result is therefore not invariance of model behavior, but containment: typed contracts, registry validation, deterministic dispatch, lineage-aware feedback, and truthful reporting made variation visible and prevented the observed incomplete plans from silently becoming unrestricted robot action.

\section{Validation Scope}

The evaluation tests whether the designed system works from a user request to a correct response or robot action. A case passes only when the request is interpreted correctly, the relevant world information is used, any required plan contains suitable skills in the correct order, the orchestrator executes only the accepted plan, and the final response agrees with the observed outcome.

The unit of analysis is one user request under a fixed software, model, world-state, and skill configuration. The main questionnaire is entered through the ROS \code{rqt} chat interface, which provides repeatable text input to the same dialogue component used by the integrated system. These runs do not claim to evaluate automatic speech recognition. Robot speech output is checked separately when it is part of the case.

Controlled skill adapters support semantic-response and software-execution claims by exposing the same call and result contracts as the robot-facing skills. Deterministic simulated outcomes provide repeatable success and failure conditions, while physical claims still require direct robot or sensor observation. The validation is organised into the following stages:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Perception and grounding:} checks that simulated or detected people and objects are represented with the correct identifiers, types, properties, and relations. In software-only runs, the people and objects are simulated by inserting controlled scene and KB facts.
  \item \textbf{Intent and routing:} checks that dialogue and knowledge questions do not request a plan, while action requests do.
  \item \textbf{Planner:} checks that an admitted action request becomes a valid sequence of available skills with the required arguments.
  \item \textbf{Skill execution:} checks ordered execution, controlled failures, replanning or clarification, world-state updates, and truthful reporting without requiring hardware.
  \item \textbf{Full end-to-end test:} checks the complete path from an \code{rqt} chat request through dialogue, grounding, planning, orchestration, skills, and the final response. Here, \emph{end-to-end} means that all software components of the designed system participate in the same case.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Skill-Execution and Failure Stress Testing}

The validation follows the same observable boundaries for robot-facing and simulated skill adapters. Robot skills provide evidence from the physical platform, whereas deterministic adapters reproduce the same result contract while allowing the outcome of an individual skill call to be selected. This makes it possible to stress planning, dispatch, feedback, replanning, and reporting without changing the request or planner-visible skill definition.

Table~\ref{tab:validation-boundary-families} groups the tests by the interaction or execution boundary they exercise. Dialogue and KB checks establish that non-executable turns remain outside the planner. Single-skill and composite cases then test the transition from grounded request to ordered execution. Failure-management cases examine whether a failed step is correlated with the active goal and produces a valid continuation or truthful termination.

Failure injection is interpreted by its position within the accepted goal, as summarised in Table~\ref{tab:failure-stage-methodology}. A failure at the beginning tests whether the initial plan is stopped before later work is dispatched. A middle-stage failure tests whether completed work and goal lineage are preserved while the remaining sequence is revised. An end-stage failure tests final action or reporting closure. The retained evidence contains controlled beginning- and middle-stage skill failures; end-stage evidence is provided by terminal-report checks rather than by a dedicated last-skill failure injection.

The expected response depends on the controlled outcome. A successful case must complete all required steps and update the represented world state. A recoverable failure may produce a revised valid plan; missing information must produce clarification; and an unrecoverable failure must produce a truthful final message. No case passes if completion is reported after a failed or unexecuted step.

\section{Metrics}

All rates in Table~\ref{tab:validation-metrics} range from 0 to 1. For every metric, 1 is the best possible result and 0 is the worst. Cases for which a metric is not applicable are excluded from that metric's denominator.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\small
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.16}
\caption{Validation metrics and their interpretation.}
\label{tab:validation-metrics}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}p{0.25\textwidth}X p{0.20\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Metric} & \textbf{Definition} & \textbf{Interpretation}\\
\midrule
Route accuracy & Fraction of cases assigned to the expected dialogue, knowledge, or execution path. & 1: every route correct; 0: none correct.\\
Valid-plan rate & Fraction of execution cases that produce a plan accepted by the orchestrator. & 1: every plan valid; 0: no plan valid.\\
Required-skill coverage & Fraction of required actions that appear in the accepted plans. & 1: no required action omitted; 0: all omitted.\\
Step-order accuracy & Fraction of ordered cases in which the required robot actions occur in the requested sequence. & 1: every sequence correct; 0: none correct.\\
Safe-failure rate & Fraction of injected failures that end in a valid replan, clarification, or truthful failure. & 1: every failure handled safely; 0: none handled safely.\\
Target-set exactness & Fraction of target-bearing cases in which the executed objects or people exactly match those requested. & 1: every target set exact; 0: none exact.\\
Role integrity & Fraction of cases in which people remain people, objects remain objects, and locations remain locations. & 1: no role errors; 0: every case has a role error.\\
Report coverage & Fraction of attempted targets or actions represented correctly in the final report. & 1: complete reporting; 0: nothing reported correctly.\\
Postcondition rate & Fraction of required world-state changes confirmed by the post-execution KB query. & 1: every change confirmed; 0: none confirmed.\\
Decision uniqueness & Fraction of turns that produce one route decision without contradictory dialogue and execution behaviour. & 1: every turn consistent; 0: every turn contradictory.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

Latency is reported separately using the median, interquartile range, 95th percentile, maximum, and timeout count. A case passes only when every applicable acceptance criterion succeeds. Any missing mandatory element, including terminal closure, is reported as a failure. A result is excluded from scoring only when the required observation is unavailable or cannot be correlated reliably.

\section{Model and Configuration Variability}

The ROS~2 interfaces and runtime contracts are model-independent, but the semantic outputs of compatible models are not assumed to be invariant. Two models can receive the same user request, grounded entity set, skill registry, and output schema while selecting different routes, target sets, arguments, or action sequences. Consequently, endpoint compatibility establishes integration compatibility only; it does not transfer task-level qualification from one model to another.

Model comparisons are interpreted only when the case set, KB fixture, skill configuration, prompt version, sampling parameters, token limits, and execution policy are held constant. Results obtained from different configurations are reported as separate evidence scopes rather than pooled into one score.

Within-model variation is evaluated through repeated prompts under the same configuration. Alternative models are screened first on demanding composite and route-consistency cases; failure on those cases is sufficient to reject equivalence for the tested configuration, but not to construct a universal ranking. A replacement model must complete the full qualification before it can be treated as operationally equivalent.

\section{Threats to Validity}

The fixed questionnaire covers only a bounded part of natural language, and repeated development against known prompts may improve performance specifically on those prompts. The robustness set reduces this risk through paraphrase and repetition but does not estimate performance over unrestricted instructions. Controlled people, objects, and relations also simplify the grounding problem compared with an uncontrolled environment.

Language-model output may vary even when the request, KB entities, prompt, and skill registry are unchanged. A single pass can therefore overstate reliability, while comparisons across different models or backend settings can confound model quality with configuration changes. The methodology addresses this by retaining all repetitions, separating configuration tuples, and limiting cross-model conclusions to matched evidence.

Deterministic simulated adapters make it possible to test the same planner, orchestrator, feedback, and reporting logic independently of the robot platform. This reduces platform dependence and supports reuse through equivalent skill adapters. Simulated results do not measure physical navigation, grasping, manipulation, or safety. Physical observations in this thesis are limited to the capabilities exercised on NAO, while the software-level conclusions apply to platforms that provide equivalent skill and result interfaces.

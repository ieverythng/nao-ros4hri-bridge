\chapter{Background and Literature Review}
\label{chap:background}

\section{Human-Robot Interaction}

Human-robot interaction (HRI) studies how people and robots communicate, coordinate tasks, and share physical or social space. In service and social robotics, an interaction cannot be reduced to command recognition. The robot must associate speech and other social signals with people, objects, prior turns, and the state of an embodied task. It must also communicate uncertainty and failure in a manner that is understandable to the user. Surveys of HRI and socially interactive robots therefore describe a multidisciplinary problem spanning robotics, artificial intelligence, perception, dialogue, interaction design, and safety \parencite{fong2003survey,goodrich2007human}.

These concerns make responsibility boundaries important. Dialogue management must preserve turn and participant context. Perception must retain the provenance and freshness of observations. Planning must operate over capabilities the robot can actually invoke, while execution must reject unsupported or unsafe work. A user-facing response must remain connected to the evidence produced by these layers. The architecture studied in this thesis treats those responsibilities as collaborating components rather than one end-to-end language policy.

\section{ROS 2 for Distributed Robot Software}

The Robot Operating System (ROS) provides the communication infrastructure used to distribute these responsibilities across robot software. Nodes exchange data through typed topics, services, and actions. Topics carry streamed observations and state, services handle bounded request-response operations, and actions support long-running or cancellable work with feedback. ROS~2 extends this model with middleware and deployment mechanisms for distributed robotic systems \parencite{quigley2009ros,macenski2022ros2}.

This communication model does not itself define HRI semantics or guarantee correct execution. Its value for this thesis lies in the ability to assign ownership to observable interfaces. Language models can remain at dialogue and task-planning boundaries, while robot actions, knowledge queries, lifecycle state, and feedback retain typed ROS~2 owners. The same separation supports controlled replacement of real robot skills with deterministic simulated outcomes during validation.

\section{Reusable HRI Representations and Interfaces}

Reusable HRI systems require consistent representations of people and their observable signals. \textcite{mohamed2020ros4hri} identify the absence of a standard human representation as a barrier to composing perception and interaction pipelines. Their ROS4HRI proposal defines conventions for bodies, faces, voices, groups, and related social signals. This thesis uses those conventions and compatible packages at the public person and speech boundaries; it does not treat ROS4HRI as the planning or execution framework of the system.

In a modular HRI system, information passes through several levels of abstraction. Sensor and speech streams first become tracked observations. Tracking and semantic sources associate those observations with people, objects, and interaction groups. A knowledge representation then exposes bounded entities and relations to dialogue and planning. User intent and grounded roles become candidate skill plans, and admitted plans become typed robot actions whose results feed the knowledge and dialogue paths. Each transition removes detail, so it must preserve the identifiers, roles, freshness, and provenance required by the next layer. An error at one transition can otherwise appear as a prompt, planning, or execution failure downstream.

\section{The NAO Robot Platform}

The physical platform used in this thesis is NAO, a programmable humanoid robot originally developed by Aldebaran Robotics. \textcite{gouaillier2008nao} describe NAO as an autonomous humanoid platform for research, education, and RoboCup experimentation. Its speech capabilities, articulated body, camera, and established academic use make it suitable for studying spoken interaction, person and object grounding, gaze-oriented actions, motion, and feedback-driven task execution. NAO is the embodied platform in the evaluated system, while higher-level components depend on reviewed skills and ROS~2 interfaces rather than direct model-generated NAOqi calls.

\section{LLMs as High-Level Planners}

Large language models have been studied as high-level planners because they encode procedural and semantic knowledge that can be elicited from natural-language instructions. \textcite{huang2022zeroshot} show that language models can decompose embodied tasks into action-like steps, while also exposing the difficulty of mapping unconstrained language to admissible actions. A linguistically reasonable plan can remain impossible, unsafe, or unsupported by the available robot.

Several approaches ground model proposals in a restricted action set. SayCan combines language-model task knowledge with affordance scores from pretrained skills \parencite{ahn2022saycan}. Code as Policies generates policy code over a supplied API, allowing feedback loops and spatial computations while remaining dependent on reviewed callable primitives \parencite{liang2022codepolicies}. These approaches motivate the use of an explicit skill registry in this thesis. The model plans over declared capabilities rather than arbitrary natural-language actions.

Reasoning-and-acting methods also show the value of external evidence. ReAct interleaves language reasoning with actions that query tools or environments \parencite{yao2022react}. Inner Monologue incorporates perception, success detectors, and human feedback into embodied planning \parencite{huang2022innermonologue}. Reflexion stores verbalised feedback as experience across trials without modifying model weights \parencite{shinn2023reflexion}. The system in this thesis adopts a narrower runtime loop: a planner emits a structured skill plan, an orchestrator validates and dispatches it, skills return typed results, and subsequent planning or dialogue acts are conditioned on that feedback.

Prompt instructions alone cannot guarantee valid skills, arguments, grounding, or execution order. The implemented stack therefore combines model output with schema validation, registry lookup, argument checks, lineage checks, and deterministic dispatch. The distinction is between using an LLM as one task-reasoning component and allowing it to become the controller of the robot.

\section{Embodied Multimodal Models, VLMs, and VLAs}

Vision-language models (VLMs) connect visual observations with language, while vision-language-action models (VLAs) also map visual and language inputs to robot actions. BLIP-2 connects frozen vision encoders and language models through a lightweight transformer \parencite{li2023blip2}. LLaVA uses visual instruction tuning to support image-conditioned interaction \parencite{liu2023llava}. These models show how visual evidence can be transformed into language-compatible representations without themselves defining a complete robot runtime.

Embodied multimodal models extend this direction. PaLM-E incorporates visual and continuous sensor inputs and evaluates them on embodied tasks \parencite{driess2023palme}. RT-2 represents robot actions as tokens and co-trains on web and robot data \parencite{brohan2023rt2}. OpenVLA provides an open-source VLA trained on a broad collection of robot demonstrations \parencite{kim2024openvla}. This thesis does not train an end-to-end VLA policy. Visual and knowledge evidence instead become compact symbolic context before reaching the dialogue and planning components. Richer spatial or multimodal grounding remains a future extension.

\section{Symbolic State and Knowledge Bases}

High-level robot reasoning requires a representation of the world. Raw perception streams are detailed and transient, while unstructured language summaries may omit identity, freshness, or relation information. A symbolic knowledge base (KB) provides one bridge between these representations. KnowRob represents objects, actions, environments, and task knowledge for robot reasoning \parencite{tenorth2013knowrob,tenorth2017knowrob}. RoboBrain similarly combines multiple knowledge sources to support grounding, perception, and planning \parencite{saxena2014robobrain}. Other work studies how interaction can help a robot acquire environmental knowledge and resolve referring expressions \parencite{bastianelli2013knowledge,grassi2021knowledge}.

The implementation follows this principle at bounded scale. Detector-derived observations, person state, and selected KB statements are projected into compact grounded context. The projection retains stable handles, semantic type, interaction role, visibility, and a bounded relation set. Dialogue and planning can use those entities without ingesting unrestricted detector payloads or RDF rows. Skills may still query fresh source evidence at execution time. This separation limits prompt growth and prevents planning-time state from being presented as proof of a later physical effect.

\section{Execution Monitoring, Replanning, and Clarification}

Robot planning cannot stop at plan generation. Actions fail, targets disappear, a user may change the goal, and safe continuation may require clarification. Feedback-oriented language-agent and embodied-planning work supports this closed-loop view \parencite{huang2022innermonologue,yao2022react,shinn2023reflexion}.

The evaluated stack treats execution feedback as a typed runtime contract. A failed or ambiguous result can stop dispatch, request user input, or return control to the planning component. The orchestrator does not create replacement plans, and the planner does not execute skills. Clarification follows the same ownership path: the planning component emits a structured communication request and the dialogue component realises the user-facing utterance. This keeps recovery observable and avoids inventing missing world facts.

\section{Literature Gap}

Prior work establishes the main ingredients separately. Language models can decompose tasks into action-like sequences \parencite{huang2022zeroshot}; affordance and API grounding can restrict proposals to available capabilities \parencite{ahn2022saycan,liang2022codepolicies}; multimodal systems can connect vision, language, and action \parencite{driess2023palme,brohan2023rt2,kim2024openvla}; symbolic systems can support robot knowledge and grounding \parencite{tenorth2013knowrob,saxena2014robobrain}; and feedback can condition later reasoning \parencite{huang2022innermonologue,yao2022react}.

The gap addressed here is the integration and validation of these responsibilities within one modular architecture. The contribution combines dialogue lifecycle, semantic route selection, compact symbolic grounding, skill-based planning, deterministic admission and dispatch, typed execution feedback, and evidence-based reporting. It occupies a middle position between fixed intent-to-skill mappings and end-to-end language or VLA policies. The architecture can express composite tasks and react to feedback, while exposing interfaces that permit each transition to be inspected independently.

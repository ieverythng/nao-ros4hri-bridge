The problem

An LLM planner call is not an interactive robot architecture.

Language is flexible

  • Users refer to people, objects, and prior turns.
  • Requests mix dialogue, knowledge, and action.
  • Plans can be fluent while incomplete or unsupported.

Robots require evidence

  • Actions need explicit capability and argument checks.
  • Execution must preserve identity, order, and lineage.
  • Success must follow typed effects, not plausible wording.
Use one simple example: asking to bring every object to ALEX requires route choice, entity roles, plan coverage, execution feedback, and final reporting.
Central research question
How can a modular robot architecture integrate language-based dialogue, symbolic grounding, task planning, deterministic execution, and feedback while preserving clear responsibility boundaries and evidence-based user communication?

Model-agnostic, not model-invariant

The same interfaces admitted different semantic behaviour.

Stable across models

Environment profile, startup ownership, symbolic transport, typed execution boundaries.

Variable across models

Composition, target selection, KB wording, clarification, plan coverage, terminal closure.

Interpretation

Backend compatibility supports portability. It does not transfer qualification.

Model-agnostic describes the interface boundary. Model-invariant would describe equivalent behaviour, which the results do not show.
This is a likely committee question. Keep the definition crisp.
Failure position

Different stages expose different obligations.

Beginning

find_object or navigation fails before useful work.

Observed: complete stop, versioned replan, target preservation.

Middle

Finding succeeds and pick_object fails.

Observed: completed work and lineage survive the revised plan.

End

Delivery boundary or final closure fails.

Observed: replanning can occur without reliable terminal speech.

State the asymmetry: end-stage evidence is about recipient and closure boundaries, not a balanced dedicated last-skill injection.

Current limitations

The strongest claims are software and symbolic-state claims.

Supported

  • interface and ownership architecture;
  • deterministic admission and lineage;
  • controlled failure and replanning;
  • symbolic postconditions and reporting.

Not established

  • navigation and grasp accuracy;
  • manipulation reliability;
  • detector performance;
  • human safety or unrestricted autonomy;
  • statistical model ranking.
Do not apologise for the boundary. Explain why controlled adapters were necessary to validate planning and recovery independently of hardware variance.

Conclusion
Language-model dialogue and planning can participate in an integrated robot architecture when their outputs are grounded, validated against reviewed skills, dispatched deterministically, and checked against typed feedback and symbolic postconditions.

The boundaries do not eliminate non-determinism. They make it observable and restrict its execution authority.

Appendix C

Short answers for likely questions

Why fake skills?They place deterministic outcomes at the robot-facing result boundary, allowing planning, recovery, postconditions, and reporting to be tested independently of hardware variance.
What is deterministic?Admission, schema validation, skill dispatch order, lineage, typed feedback, and effect verification. The generated route and plan remain model-dependent.
Why model-agnostic?The interfaces and adapters accept multiple backends. The results explicitly show that semantic behaviour is not invariant.
Why not classical planning?The work focuses on flexible language interpretation and interactive decomposition, while retaining deterministic checks that classical planners make explicit.
What comes next?Physical skill trials with quantified safety and performance, then perception and world-model expansion under the same contracts.

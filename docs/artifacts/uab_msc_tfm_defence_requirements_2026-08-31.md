## Confirmed requirements

### Defence sequence and duration

- The oral presentation must last **between 25 and 30 minutes**.
- The jury's question and discussion period occurs **after the presentation** and may last **up to 30 minutes**.
- The resulting public session can therefore occupy as much as **60 minutes**, excluding any private jury deliberation or room turnover that may follow. Neither source specifies the duration of deliberation.
- The guide describes a sequential format, presentation first and jury discussion afterwards. It does not state that jury members may interrupt the prepared presentation.

Source: [2025/2026 teaching guide, pp. 2–3](https://ddd.uab.cat/pub/procur/2025-26/g42257a2025-26iENG.pdf).

### Required presentation content

The programme guide explicitly instructs the student to use the presentation to:

1. frame the question or problem;
2. state the objectives;
3. explain the work and results;
4. place the results in context; and
5. present the conclusions.

The stated learning outcomes add the following assessed capabilities. These should be visible in the presentation even though the guide does not prescribe named slides:

- communicate procedures and results to both expert and general audiences;
- identify the programming environments, optimisation or machine-learning methods, computing architectures, and mathematical tools used for the project;
- show the use of specific or self-developed software;
- interpret the results;
- critically identify advantages, disadvantages, and limitations of the methods;
- assess whether the tools can transfer to broader industrial or research problems; and
- address relevant ethical, sustainability, gender-equality, or social-justice implications.

Source: [2025/2026 teaching guide, learning outcomes and presentation instructions](https://apps.uab.cat/guies/public/portal/html/2025/assignatura/42257/en).

### Public character of the defence

- The defence is a **public presentation** of the written master's thesis.
- The public guide assigns the question period to the jury. It does not specify whether other attendees may ask questions.

Source: [2025/2026 teaching guide, objectives and contextualisation](https://ddd.uab.cat/pub/procur/2025-26/g42257a2025-26iENG.pdf).

### Tribunal composition

- The programme-specific tribunal consists of **three members**.
- The thesis supervisor, or a member of the supervisor's team, may sit on the tribunal but **must not chair it**.
- At least **one tribunal member must belong to the UAB**.
- The date and time are arranged after the members have accepted their appointment.

The Faculty of Sciences process provides a general two-or-three-member range, but the programme guide is more specific and therefore the three-member requirement controls for module 42257. Neither source requires all three members to be UAB staff or publicly states a doctoral-degree requirement for this programme's tribunal.

Sources: [2025/2026 teaching guide, assessment](https://ddd.uab.cat/pub/procur/2025-26/g42257a2025-26iENG.pdf) and [Faculty process PC2.02, section 8.4](https://www.uab.cat/ca/ciencies/doc/pc202.pdf).

### Assessment components

The final module grade is divided as follows:

| Component | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Content and execution of the work itself | 50% |
| Written report | 30% |
| Oral dissertation and defence | 20% |

The public guide does not provide a finer point-by-point rubric for the 20% oral component. Its learning outcomes are therefore the most specific published indicators of what the oral defence is expected to demonstrate.

Source: [2025/2026 teaching guide, assessment](https://apps.uab.cat/guies/public/portal/html/2025/assignatura/42257/en).

### Submission process

- During March or April, the programme was to enable a Campus Virtual task in which each student reported the thesis title, adviser, and abstract.
- The governing guide required the TFM report to be sent through the subject's Virtual Campus by **21 June 2026 at 23:59**.
- The Faculty of Sciences process makes digital receipt of the written report mandatory and identifies **PDF** as the archive/submission format, with a printed submission only where applicable or exceptionally required.
- No public source found requires the presentation deck itself to be uploaded in advance.

Sources: [2025/2026 teaching guide, calendar and delivery](https://ddd.uab.cat/pub/procur/2025-26/g42257a2025-26iENG.pdf) and [Faculty process PC2.02, sections 8.2–8.4](https://www.uab.cat/ca/ciencies/doc/pc202.pdf).

### Scheduling

- The 2025/2026 guide defines **29 June to 3 July 2026** as the main presentation period.
- The programme schedules the individual date and time after the tribunal members accept their appointment.
- The Faculty process requires each programme to establish the defence calendar and the relevant delivery dates.

The student's stated **3 September 2026** defence is not listed in the public 2025/2026 guide. It should be treated as an individually communicated appointment from the programme, supervisor, tribunal, Campus Virtual, or Academic Management. That private notification supersedes the public main-period statement for this defence, but it should be checked for the exact start time, room, access procedure, and any local file-delivery instruction.

Sources: [2025/2026 teaching guide](https://apps.uab.cat/guies/public/portal/html/2025/assignatura/42257/en) and [Faculty process PC2.02, section 8.4](https://www.uab.cat/ca/ciencies/doc/pc202.pdf).

## Not publicly specified

The official sources reviewed do **not** publicly specify the following for this programme and cohort:

- a mandatory number of slides;
- a PowerPoint, Keynote, PDF, or Google Slides requirement for the deck;
- a deadline or portal for submitting the presentation file;
- a programme-specific presentation template;
- mandatory UAB, Faculty of Sciences, IIIA-CSIC, or ROS4HRI branding on slides;
- a required slide aspect ratio;
- a required citation style inside the slides;
- whether a live demonstration is permitted, expected, or discouraged;
- whether videos must be embedded or may be played externally;
- whether backup copies must be carried on USB or provided to the panel;
- the exact defence language;
- the exact September room, building, or online link;
- whether the September session is in person, hybrid, or online;
- the names or specialist backgrounds of the three tribunal members;
- whether audience members may ask questions;
- whether the panel may interrupt during the prepared presentation;
- the duration or visibility of the jury's deliberation;
- whether the grade is announced immediately; or
- a detailed oral-presentation marking rubric beneath the published 20% weight.

The official programme is taught in English and is described as classroom-based. English is consequently the safest presentation language unless the student's appointment notice or coordinator explicitly authorises another language. This is a preparation recommendation, not a published defence-language rule.

## Presentation-planning implications

The following are recommendations derived from the confirmed requirements, not additional UAB rules:

- Rehearse for **27–28 minutes**, leaving a small margin within the permitted 25–30-minute interval.
- Build the narrative around the required sequence: problem, objectives, method and system, validation evidence, contextualised results, limitations, and conclusions.
- Make the methods and software understandable to a mixed tribunal. Define ROS 2, ROS4HRI, the knowledge base, the planner, and the execution harness before using internal interface names.
- Give the validation design and canonical results more time than the development chronology. The work itself carries 50% of the overall module grade, and the oral component is expected to interpret and contextualise those results.
- Include one concise limitations and transferability section, plus a short treatment of ethical or social implications, because these appear explicitly in the module learning outcomes.
- Prepare for up to **30 minutes of technical questioning**, including architecture ownership, validity of the evaluation framework, fake-skill versus physical-robot evidence, failure recovery, reproducibility, limitations, and future work.
- Export both a `.pptx` working copy and a self-contained PDF backup, and carry all videos locally. This is risk control rather than a UAB requirement.

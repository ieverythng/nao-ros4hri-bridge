I am applying for the Software Engineer for HPC+ML Applications position
because it brings together three areas that have shaped my development:
physical modelling, machine learning, and reliable software workflows. I hold a
BSc in Mechanical Engineering and am completing an MSc in Modelling for Science
and Engineering at the Universitat Autonoma de Barcelona, specialising in Data
Science. My professional and research work now focuses on structured AI systems,
while my earlier training and engineering experience remain grounded in
physical models and numerical analysis.

My BSc thesis built and validated a neural-network model for predicting
turbulent intensity from wind-profile data. The project required connecting a
physical problem with data preparation, model design, validation, and
interpretation rather than treating machine learning as an isolated exercise.
During my engineering degree and later at ABS Consulting, I also worked with
ANSYS Fluent, Abaqus, Mathcad, Inventor, and STAAD.Pro. At ABS, I supported
seismic qualification and structural assessments for UK nuclear power assets,
where reviewable calculations, standards compliance, and technical reporting
were essential.

At Aily Labs, I have worked from prompt-defined MCP capabilities and gated
skills through semantic SQL-agent access and production evaluation. Approved
table descriptions and usage guidance provide bounded access to enterprise
data; package-owned question sets, deterministic validators, traces, and
LLM-as-a-judge scoring then measure whether the agent used the right data and
tools. Working across the Super Agent, MCP and data services has strengthened
my Python engineering, CI/CD, debugging, and ability to attribute failures
across a distributed application.

Through a JAE Intro ICU 2025 scholarship at IIIA-CSIC, I also work on a ROS 2
architecture connecting dialogue, symbolic world state, language-model
planning, deterministic execution, and trace-based evaluation for the NAO
robot. The stack has reinforced the same scientific-software principle:
results need typed inputs, observable execution, and evidence that separates a
model error from an integration or measurement error.

My principal personal ML project, iTrader, is an RLVR-style trading research
platform whose implemented baseline combines market-window simulation,
validated task curricula, an RLlib PPO/GAE training loop, and
task-conditioned FiLM policy and value networks. Its reward registry keeps PnL,
drawdown and turnover components finite, decomposed, normalised across relevant
scales, and separately observable. Local LLM proposer backends and deterministic
fallbacks generate task candidates, while run manifests retain code,
configuration, seed, and dataset identity. The intended route to live trading
adds capability-profiled solver heads, grouped proposer optimisation,
Market-JEPA representations, and replay or shadow evaluation before
deterministic risk and execution.

The Watson Stack integrates local llama.cpp serving, LiteLLM routing, and
tool-based agent workflows. My unpublished Universal Agentic Harness H0 proof
studies typed task and capability contracts, portable adapters, deterministic
evidence gates, and replayable traces. These projects are not substitutes for
production HPC experience, but they demonstrate how I approach computational
systems: explicit interfaces, bounded experiments, observable execution, and
documentation that supports reproducible iteration.

The Wave Phenomena role is an ambitious application for my current level. I do
not yet claim expert C/C++ programming, leadership of European projects, or
three to five years in a directly comparable HPC position. My experience with
large-scale HPC facilities, GPU porting, and distributed scientific software is
also developing rather than established. I am applying because my combination
of physical modelling, machine learning, Python systems work, and rigorous
engineering practice offers a relevant foundation, and because the Earth-AID
and geoscience focus is a compelling setting in which to deepen those
capabilities.

I would bring curiosity, disciplined validation, clear communication, and
experience moving between domain experts and software abstractions. BSC's
ability to unite scientific research, modern computational infrastructure, and
real engineering applications makes it an exceptional environment for that
next step.

Thank you for considering my application. I would welcome a discussion about
whether my interdisciplinary background and growth trajectory fit the needs of
the Wave Phenomena group.

Yours sincerely,

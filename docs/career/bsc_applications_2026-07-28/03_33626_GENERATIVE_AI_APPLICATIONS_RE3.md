I am applying for the AI Engineer - Generative AI Applications position because
the AI Factory's objective of turning validated AI concepts into reliable,
reusable applications closely reflects the work I want to continue developing.
My background combines enterprise agent systems, research robotics,
computational modelling, and engineering in a safety-sensitive domain. I am
currently completing an MSc in Modelling for Science and Engineering at the
Universitat Autonoma de Barcelona, specialising in Data Science.

At Aily Labs, I have worked across the lifecycle by which a language model
becomes a production analytical application. Prompt-defined MCP capabilities
and a server package for go-to-market workflows evolved towards gated skills,
where the runtime controls which tools and instructions are available for a
task. A semantic layer then provides approved SQL table descriptions, business
meaning, and usage guidance to an SQL agent, giving it direct but bounded
access to enterprise information.

The same work now extends into evaluation and release discipline. Package-owned
question sets define expected tables, skills, tools, and answer criteria;
benchmark runs combine deterministic validators, traces, and LLM-as-a-judge
scoring into an Agent IQ measure. I have therefore worked with failures across
semantic context, routing, tool selection, SQL behaviour, service integration,
and CI/CD rather than treating every poor answer as a prompt problem. These
experiences are directly relevant to context management, tool calling,
observability, evaluation, and the continuous improvement of reusable AI
services before or alongside model post-training.

At IIIA-CSIC, my JAE Intro ICU 2025 scholarship and MSc thesis concern a modular
language-model-based architecture for the NAO robot. The ROS 2 stack separates
dialogue, grounded symbolic context, structured planning, deterministic action
dispatch, execution feedback, and user reporting. My contribution has included
cross-component contracts, planner and skill interfaces, failure handling,
trace capture, and a validation methodology spanning dialogue, knowledge-base
queries, simple and composite actions, and controlled recovery. This work has
made reliability concrete: an application should expose where a decision came
from, what capability was invoked, what evidence was returned, and whether the
final statement is supported.

My earlier work at ABS Consulting adds an engineering perspective that I
consider valuable for production AI. Supporting seismic qualification and
structural assessments for nuclear power assets required disciplined modelling,
standards-based reasoning, reviewable calculations, and precise technical
reports. My BSc thesis also connected machine learning with physical modelling
through neural-network prediction of turbulent intensity from wind-profile
data.

My personal work examines the same reliability problem from the model and
harness sides. iTrader is an RLVR-style trading research stack with an
implemented task-conditioned PPO/GAE solver, FiLM policy and value networks,
validated task specifications, decomposed and normalised reward components,
local LLM proposer backends, and reproducible experiment manifests. It is
intended to become a live platform, but the planned route passes through
capability-profiled solver heads, grouped proposer optimisation, Market-JEPA
representations, replay and shadow modes, and deterministic risk gates before
execution.

The unpublished Universal Agentic Harness H0 proof provides the systems
counterpart. Its abstraction-boundary model compiles a minimal task-specific
view of capabilities, context, permissions, and evidence rather than exposing a
global tool set. It currently implements focused contract tests, registry
projection, deterministic output gates, append-only traces, and environment
adapters, but is not live-integrated or externally validated. The Watson Stack
adds local model serving and tool workflows through LiteLLM, llama.cpp, and a
Hermes-style loop.

I recognise that this RE3 vacancy requests a longer record of production
software and LLM delivery than my current title history alone demonstrates. I
am nevertheless applying because the technical overlap is substantial and my
trajectory has been unusually concentrated across the required boundaries:
Python AI applications, agent and tool interfaces, structured context,
evaluation, observability, documentation, and multidisciplinary delivery. I
would bring high ownership, careful systems thinking, and the humility to treat
cloud deployment, large-scale RAG, security, and LLMOps depth as areas for
continued growth rather than overstating them.

BSC's combination of scientific infrastructure, public-interest AI, and
cross-domain engineering is exactly the environment in which I want to build.
I would welcome the opportunity to discuss how my experience could contribute
to trustworthy generative AI services and reusable AI Factory components.

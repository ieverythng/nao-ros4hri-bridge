Dear Members of the Recruitment Panel,

I am writing to express my interest in the PhD position on high-fidelity
hypersonic flows within BSC's Large-Scale Computational Fluid Dynamics team. My
background is in mechanical and process engineering rather than aeronautical
engineering, but the project's combination of turbulence modelling,
machine-learning methods, numerical simulation, and high-performance computing
closely matches the interdisciplinary research direction I want to pursue.

I completed a BSc in Mechanical Engineering at the Budapest University of
Technology and Economics under a Stipendium Hungaricum scholarship, with a
Process Engineering specialisation and a final grade of 4.33. I am now
completing an MSc in Modelling for Science and Engineering at the Universitat
Autonoma de Barcelona, specialising in Data Science. My BSc thesis built and
validated a neural-network model for turbulent-intensity prediction from
wind-profile data. That work gave me direct experience joining fluid-mechanical
reasoning with data preparation, model development, validation, and careful
interpretation of approximation limits.

My engineering training included ANSYS Fluent and other numerical and CAD/FEA
tools. At ABS Consulting, I supported seismic qualification and structural
assessment projects for UK nuclear power assets using Abaqus, Mathcad, Inventor,
and STAAD.Pro. The work required controlled calculations, standards-based
reasoning, reviewable technical reports, and communication with engineers
working under demanding assurance requirements. These habits are highly
relevant to research in which numerical accuracy, reproducibility, and physical
interpretation cannot be separated.

My current work has moved deeper into AI and software systems. At Aily Labs, I
have worked from prompt-defined MCP capabilities and gated skills through
semantic SQL-agent access and production evaluation. The evaluation layer uses
curated question sets, declared data and tool expectations, trace inspection,
and LLM-as-a-judge scoring. This has strengthened my Python engineering, CI/CD,
experimental design, and ability to distinguish model limitations from data or
system-integration faults.

Through a JAE Intro ICU 2025 scholarship at IIIA-CSIC, my MSc thesis focuses on
a modular ROS 2 architecture for interactive robotics, including symbolic
grounding, structured language-model planning, deterministic execution,
feedback, and controlled evaluation. Although this is a different application
domain, it has reinforced my approach to machine-learning components whose
outputs must remain constrained by physical or execution evidence.

My personal iTrader project provides a more training-intensive research
environment. Its implemented RLVR-style baseline uses market-window simulation,
validated task specifications, PPO/GAE, task-conditioned FiLM networks, and
finite decomposed rewards. The longer-term architecture introduces
capability-profiled solver heads, grouped proposer optimisation, and latent
Market-JEPA representations before progressing through replay, shadow mode,
deterministic risk, and eventual live execution. The domain differs from CFD,
but the methodological concerns are shared: scale-sensitive objectives,
distribution shift, reproducible runs, and evaluation beyond a single aggregate
score.

I want to be precise about my present fit. I have used CFD software and studied
turbulence-related machine learning, but I have not yet developed an in-house
hypersonic CFD solver or led exascale GPU simulations. My direct GPU-porting and
large-scale HPC experience is limited. I therefore view this position as a PhD
training path into high-Mach LES, wall modelling, GPU-aware numerical methods,
and scientific software, not as an area in which I already claim specialist
status. I would be grateful for the panel's consideration of whether my
mechanical/process engineering degree is accepted as equivalent to the listed
aeronautical background.

What I would bring is a strong mathematical and physical foundation, practical
engineering discipline, experience applying machine learning to a turbulence
problem, and sustained evidence of learning across fields. I have studied and
worked in multinational environments in Hungary, the United Kingdom, and Spain,
and I work fluently in English and Spanish. I am motivated by collaborative
research and by the opportunity to contribute to publishable work that connects
physical models, numerical methods, machine learning, and modern computing
architectures.

BSC's LS/CFD programme offers exactly the environment in which these interests
can be developed rigorously. I would welcome the opportunity to discuss my
eligibility and how my background could contribute to the high-speed research
activities of the group.

Yours sincerely,
